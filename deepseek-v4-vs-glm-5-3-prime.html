The bottom line

On the two vendors' own benchmark tables, DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813 and GLM-5.3-Prime are close enough that neither wins outright. The real differences are not on the leaderboard but in three things: price (DeepSeek off-peak is roughly 1/4.3 of Prime for the same job), maximum single output (384K vs about 128K), and deployment shape (both families publish weights, but Prime itself is an API serving tier).

  • Lowest unit cost and the largest single output → DeepSeek V4-Pro (or the cheaper V4.1-Flash).
  • Higher streaming throughput for realtime coding or long-horizon agents → GLM-5.3-Prime.
  • Multimodal input at 1M context → DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (V4-Pro does not support vision).

Worked example: 1M input (cache miss) + 250K output

  • DeepSeek V4-Pro (off-peak): $0.66 + 0.25 × $1.98 ≈ $1.16
  • DeepSeek V4-Pro (peak): $1.32 + 0.25 × $3.96 ≈ $2.31
  • DeepSeek V4.1-Flash (off-peak): $0.15 + 0.25 × $0.60 ≈ $0.30
  • GLM-5.3-Prime: $2.80 + 0.25 × $8.80 = $5.00

For the same job, DeepSeek V4-Pro off-peak is about 1/4.3 of Prime and even at peak costs roughly half; V4.1-Flash is another order of magnitude cheaper. Cache-heavy workloads widen the gap further (25× on the cache-hit rate).

⚡ What Prime Actually Accelerates

GLM-5.3-Prime is not a new model: it is a high-speed serving tier of GLM-5.3. Z.ai and OpenRouter both describe it as inheriting GLM-5.3's full capabilities while inference acceleration lifts output throughput to roughly 1.5–2×, with third-party measurements around 77 tokens/s. Its selling point is streaming feel — realtime coding chat, streamed code generation, and long-horizon agents that already wanted GLM-5.3 quality but found the base ID too slow.

DeepSeek has no equivalent accelerated tier; it buys the same outcome a different way, splitting the day into peak and off-peak windows at half price and publishing concurrency limits (500 for Pro, 2500 for Flash). So these are two different optimisation paths: GLM optimises tokens per second, DeepSeek optimises dollars per million tokens.

🎯 When to Pick Which

💰

Cost-sensitive, high volume

Go DeepSeek. Half-price off-peak plus a cache-hit rate of $0.022/MTok against $0.56 compounds into multiples on long-running batch work.

⌨️

Realtime coding, streaming chat

Go GLM-5.3-Prime. Its whole reason to exist is faster token output, which pays off most where a human is watching the stream.

📄

Very long single outputs

Go DeepSeek V4. Its 384K single-output ceiling is roughly three times Prime's ~128K, so whole-repo generation or book-length rewrites do not hit the wall.

🖼️

You need image input

Neither Pro tier takes images. On the DeepSeek side use V4.1-Flash (vision-capable), or move to a natively multimodal model such as Claude Sonnet 5.5.

❓ FAQ

What is the difference between GLM 5.3 Prime and GLM 5.3?

Prime is a high-speed serving tier of GLM-5.3, not a new model: same capability band, roughly 1.5–2× output throughput from inference acceleration, measured at about 77 tokens/s by third parties. Its benchmark numbers are the GLM-5.3 numbers — Terminal Bench 2.1 at 88.2, for example. The model ID is z-ai/glm-5.3-prime.

Is DeepSeek V4 or GLM 5.3 Prime stronger?

On the 2026 tables the two vendors published, they are very close: Terminal Bench 2.1 is 87.9 vs 88.2, CyberGym 83.3 vs 84.5, Toolathlon Verified 74.1 vs 73.0, NL2Repo 61.5 vs 58.0. Which one leads depends on the benchmark, and both are vendor-run under different harnesses, so this is not an absolute ranking. The real differences are price, maximum output length and deployment shape.

Do both have open weights?

GLM-5.3 weights are on Hugging Face (zai-org/GLM-5.3, with Flash and BF16 variants) under Z.ai's own glm-5.3 licence, but GLM-5.3-Prime is an API serving tier with no separate weights. DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813 weights are also published, under an MIT licence, alongside V4.1-Flash in the same family.

Are both really 1M-token context?

Both are listed at roughly 1M tokens of context in the official docs. Maximum single output differs, though: DeepSeek V4 allows up to 384K output tokens versus about 128K for GLM-5.3-Prime. If your workload is one long generation, that gap matters more than the window itself.

How big is the price gap on the same job?

List prices for 1M input (cache miss) plus 250K output: DeepSeek V4-Pro is about $1.16 off-peak and $2.31 at peak, V4.1-Flash about $0.30 off-peak, and GLM-5.3-Prime $5.00. Off-peak, DeepSeek V4-Pro runs at about 1/4.3 of Prime; where caching applies, the cache-hit rate differs by 25×.
